Overview

The video introduces the transformer architecture, the backbone of modern large language models. Instead of processing text one word at a time like older recurrent networks, transformers look at an entire sequence at once using a mechanism called self-attention.

Self-attention, in one sentence

Every word produces a query, key, and value vector; attention scores measure how relevant every other word is to the current one, and the output is a weighted blend of all the value vectors.

Metaphor Think of self-attention as a room full of people at a conference: each person (word) briefly "listens" to everyone else in the room and adjusts what they say next based on who seemed most relevant to their own topic.

Why it scales well

  • All positions are processed in parallel, unlike RNNs which are sequential.
  • Positional encoding injects word order back in, since attention itself is order-agnostic.
  • Stacking many attention layers lets the model build increasingly abstract representations.

Worked example

For the sentence "the cat sat because it was tired", attention lets the model connect it to cat rather than sat, resolving the pronoun correctly using context from across the whole sentence.
